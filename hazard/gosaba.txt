HAZARD BRIEFING: GOSABA
============================================================

Royal Bengal Tiger: Low risk of tiger straying/encounter in this forest block, based on peer-reviewed casualty studies. Risk is highest during pre-monsoon (April-June) and early winter (January-March). Inhabited town — not a forest-entry zone. For boat-based tourists staying on marked watchtower routes, actual risk is low — recorded incidents mainly involve fishermen, honey collectors, and crab collectors entering the deep forest, not tourists on guided boats.

Crocodile: Low-Moderate risk near creek edges and water access points. Regional data shows attacks peak in winter (December-February) and early monsoon (May-July), with risk rising further during high tide when crocodiles move closer to banks. Some risk along the town's river edge, historically the highest-recorded block for incidents, though mainly affecting local fishers, not tourists. Avoid standing at the water's edge near watchtowers, especially during rising tide.

Snakes: Low risk, particularly during monsoon (June-September) when flooding pushes snakes out of burrows onto the same raised paths tourists use. Developed area with limited forest cover. Wear ankle-covering, closed footwear at all times outside the boat.

High Tide / Tidal Risk: Low — boat schedules at this stop are tide-dependent, not fixed-clock. Road and ferry access reduce tide dependency. Confirm departure timing with the boatman directly rather than relying on a printed schedule; boats can be stranded on mudflats if timing is misjudged.

Soft Mud / Sinking Ground: Low risk near jetty steps and mudflats, worse after rain. Paved/maintained areas in the town center. Mangrove pneumatophores (breathing roots) also create sharp trip hazards just off marked wooden paths — never step off the designated plank path.

Monsoon Weather: Moderate risk of sudden squalls, heavy rain, and rough river conditions during June-September. Storms can turn calm conditions violent within 30-40 minutes. Still subject to regional storm and rainfall patterns. Boat operations may be suspended with little notice during heavy weather.

Mosquitoes & Insects: Moderate risk, rising with monsoon humidity. Standard monsoon-season exposure. Sundarban is a malaria-prone zone — carry repellent and consult a doctor about prophylaxis before traveling, especially for multi-day trips.

Boat / Water Transport: Low risk related to vessel availability and safety. Well-established ferry routes. Always confirm the boat is registered with West Bengal Tourism or the Forest Department before booking; avoid unlicensed operators.

Network Connectivity: Good — last point with reliable network before the forest zones. Download offline maps and emergency information before reaching this zone; do not rely on live internet access here.

Getting Lost / Navigation: Low risk in the surrounding creek network, which has many visually similar channels. Town with clear roads and signage. Never allow the boat to separate from a known guide or licensed boatman, even for short detours.
