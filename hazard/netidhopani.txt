HAZARD BRIEFING: NETIDHOPANI
============================================================

Royal Bengal Tiger: Moderate risk of tiger straying/encounter in this forest block, based on peer-reviewed casualty studies. Risk is highest during pre-monsoon (April-June) and early winter (January-March). Remote forest block with regular tiger presence near the temple ruins. For boat-based tourists staying on marked watchtower routes, actual risk is low — recorded incidents mainly involve fishermen, honey collectors, and crab collectors entering the deep forest, not tourists on guided boats.

Crocodile: Moderate risk near creek edges and water access points. Regional data shows attacks peak in winter (December-February) and early monsoon (May-July), with risk rising further during high tide when crocodiles move closer to banks. Watchtower creek has recorded crocodile activity. Avoid standing at the water's edge near watchtowers, especially during rising tide.

Snakes: Moderate-High risk, particularly during monsoon (June-September) when flooding pushes snakes out of burrows onto the same raised paths tourists use. Overgrown ruins area is a known snake habitat. Wear ankle-covering, closed footwear at all times outside the boat.

High Tide / Tidal Risk: High — boat schedules at this stop are tide-dependent, not fixed-clock. One of the farthest stops — tide miscalculation risks a long stranded wait. Confirm departure timing with the boatman directly rather than relying on a printed schedule; boats can be stranded on mudflats if timing is misjudged.

Soft Mud / Sinking Ground: Moderate risk near jetty steps and mudflats, worse after rain. Ruins site access path is uneven and slippery when wet. Mangrove pneumatophores (breathing roots) also create sharp trip hazards just off marked wooden paths — never step off the designated plank path.

Monsoon Weather: High risk of sudden squalls, heavy rain, and rough river conditions during June-September. Storms can turn calm conditions violent within 30-40 minutes. Most remote core stop — longest return journey if weather turns. Boat operations may be suspended with little notice during heavy weather.

Mosquitoes & Insects: Moderate-High risk, rising with monsoon humidity. Ruins and surrounding forest hold standing water after rain. Sundarban is a malaria-prone zone — carry repellent and consult a doctor about prophylaxis before traveling, especially for multi-day trips.

Boat / Water Transport: Moderate risk related to vessel availability and safety. Longest boat transit of the core circuit — plan buffer time. Always confirm the boat is registered with West Bengal Tourism or the Forest Department before booking; avoid unlicensed operators.

Network Connectivity: Poor — no signal — the most isolated of the core stops. Download offline maps and emergency information before reaching this zone; do not rely on live internet access here.

Getting Lost / Navigation: Moderate-High risk in the surrounding creek network, which has many visually similar channels. Furthest creek approach with multiple similar channels. Never allow the boat to separate from a known guide or licensed boatman, even for short detours.
