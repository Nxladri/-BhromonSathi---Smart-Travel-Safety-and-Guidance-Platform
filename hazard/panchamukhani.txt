HAZARD BRIEFING: PANCHAMUKHANI
============================================================

Royal Bengal Tiger: Moderate risk of tiger straying/encounter in this forest block, based on peer-reviewed casualty studies. Risk is highest during pre-monsoon (April-June) and early winter (January-March). Deep-forest river confluence, not a livelihood/tourist walking zone but near known forest blocks. For boat-based tourists staying on marked watchtower routes, actual risk is low — recorded incidents mainly involve fishermen, honey collectors, and crab collectors entering the deep forest, not tourists on guided boats.

Crocodile: Moderate-High risk near creek edges and water access points. Regional data shows attacks peak in winter (December-February) and early monsoon (May-July), with risk rising further during high tide when crocodiles move closer to banks. Confluence of five channels increases water-edge encounter probability. Avoid standing at the water's edge near watchtowers, especially during rising tide.

High Tide / Tidal Risk: High — boat schedules at this stop are tide-dependent, not fixed-clock. Multiple converging tidal channels make timing especially complex here. Confirm departure timing with the boatman directly rather than relying on a printed schedule; boats can be stranded on mudflats if timing is misjudged.

Monsoon Weather: High risk of sudden squalls, heavy rain, and rough river conditions during June-September. Storms can turn calm conditions violent within 30-40 minutes. Open confluence is highly exposed to wind and sudden weather shifts. Boat operations may be suspended with little notice during heavy weather.

Mosquitoes & Insects: Moderate risk, rising with monsoon humidity. Standard monsoon exposure over open water. Sundarban is a malaria-prone zone — carry repellent and consult a doctor about prophylaxis before traveling, especially for multi-day trips.

Boat / Water Transport: Moderate-High risk related to vessel availability and safety. Complex currents at the confluence require experienced boat handling. Always confirm the boat is registered with West Bengal Tourism or the Forest Department before booking; avoid unlicensed operators.

Network Connectivity: Poor — zero signal — deep interior river route. Download offline maps and emergency information before reaching this zone; do not rely on live internet access here.

Getting Lost / Navigation: High risk in the surrounding creek network, which has many visually similar channels. Five converging channels are a well-known point of route confusion. Never allow the boat to separate from a known guide or licensed boatman, even for short detours.
