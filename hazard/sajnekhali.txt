HAZARD BRIEFING: SAJNEKHALI
============================================================

Royal Bengal Tiger: Moderate risk of tiger straying/encounter in this forest block, based on peer-reviewed casualty studies. Risk is highest during pre-monsoon (April-June) and early winter (January-March). This is a core forest-entry watchtower zone with regular tiger movement nearby. For boat-based tourists staying on marked watchtower routes, actual risk is low — recorded incidents mainly involve fishermen, honey collectors, and crab collectors entering the deep forest, not tourists on guided boats.

Crocodile: Moderate risk near creek edges and water access points. Regional data shows attacks peak in winter (December-February) and early monsoon (May-July), with risk rising further during high tide when crocodiles move closer to banks. The mangrove interpretation centre pond and creek edge are known crocodile habitat. Avoid standing at the water's edge near watchtowers, especially during rising tide.

Snakes: Moderate risk, particularly during monsoon (June-September) when flooding pushes snakes out of burrows onto the same raised paths tourists use. Ground-level paths near the watchtower flood easily in monsoon. Wear ankle-covering, closed footwear at all times outside the boat.

High Tide / Tidal Risk: Moderate — boat schedules at this stop are tide-dependent, not fixed-clock. Main forest-entry jetty; tide timing affects both arrival and return. Confirm departure timing with the boatman directly rather than relying on a printed schedule; boats can be stranded on mudflats if timing is misjudged.

Soft Mud / Sinking Ground: Moderate risk near jetty steps and mudflats, worse after rain. Jetty steps are a known slip hazard in wet conditions. Mangrove pneumatophores (breathing roots) also create sharp trip hazards just off marked wooden paths — never step off the designated plank path.

Monsoon Weather: Moderate-High risk of sudden squalls, heavy rain, and rough river conditions during June-September. Storms can turn calm conditions violent within 30-40 minutes. Open water crossing to reach this zone is exposed to sudden squalls. Boat operations may be suspended with little notice during heavy weather.

Mosquitoes & Insects: Moderate risk, rising with monsoon humidity. Dense mangrove cover near the interpretation centre increases exposure. Sundarban is a malaria-prone zone — carry repellent and consult a doctor about prophylaxis before traveling, especially for multi-day trips.

Boat / Water Transport: Low risk related to vessel availability and safety. This is an official, well-regulated forest department entry point. Always confirm the boat is registered with West Bengal Tourism or the Forest Department before booking; avoid unlicensed operators.

Network Connectivity: Poor — drops to zero shortly after departing Gosaba. Download offline maps and emergency information before reaching this zone; do not rely on live internet access here.

Getting Lost / Navigation: Low risk in the surrounding creek network, which has many visually similar channels. Main entry channel is well-marked and frequently traveled. Never allow the boat to separate from a known guide or licensed boatman, even for short detours.
