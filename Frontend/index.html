EXPLORE THE DELTA

Discover Sundarbans

History, wildlife, local life and culture — know the land before you enter it.

🏛️

Why Sundarbans is Famous

The world’s largest mangrove forest and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. From early settlements to modern conservation, it remains a unique tidal ecosystem of creeks, islands, and the legendary Royal Bengal Tiger.

🐯

Royal Bengal Tiger

Home to swimming tigers, crocodiles, deer and rich biodiversity. Stay on guided routes for safety.

🍯

Honey Collectors’ Risk

Local Mouli face real danger from tigers, crocodiles and terrain while collecting wild honey in deep forest routes.

🌊

Flood & Cyclone Risk

The delta faces cyclones and tidal surges. Mangroves act as a natural shield for inland communities.

🚤

Travel & Tourism

Most exploration is by licensed boat through tidal creeks and watchtowers like Sajnekhali and Dobanki.

🐅

Tiger Behavior

Sundarbans tigers are strong swimmers adapted to brackish water and dense mangrove terrain.

🌡️

Climate Change

Rising seas, salinity and erosion threaten land and mangrove cover across the delta.

🙏

Folk Legends & Culture

Shared traditions around Bonbibi reflect the deep bond between forest communities and the jungle.

🐟

Ilish & River Culture

Hilsa is central to Bengal’s food heritage and seasonal river festivals across the delta.

KNOW BEFORE YOU GO

Hazard Awareness

Quick field guides for the most important risks in the mangrove delta.

🐯

Royal Bengal Tiger

Never walk alone at dawn or dusk. Stay away from water edges. Follow guide instructions.

🐊

Estuarine Crocodile

Do not swim or wade. Keep limbs inside boats. Watch floating logs carefully.

🐍

Venomous Snakes

Wear high boots on trails. Probe leaf litter carefully. Carry basic first-aid readiness.

🦟

Mosquitoes & Insects

Use repellent. Prefer full sleeves after evening. Consider medical advice for longer stays.

🌿

Mangrove Terrain

Soft mud and roots can trap feet. Stay on marked boardwalks and designated paths.
